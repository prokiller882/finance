- (event) create intent confirmed; run folder + brief skeleton + memlog created; starting discovery, one question at a time
- (note) user dump: personal finance tracker; expenses/income by category; plan spending and income to stay within budget; sole user today; hopes to commercialize later
- (note) user's differentiator claim: mainstream trackers take an unserious approach; his app is company-grade financial management for an individual
- (note) user: reports are just the tip of the iceberg; their purpose is to structure spending so cash gaps do not happen
- (note) concrete pain: spent all funds 7 days before payday, no money for groceries, took a small loan
- (insight) gap is not data but derived answer: apps show expenses + balance, but not a daily safe-to-spend budget; that missing number is the core job
- (note) user's daily-limit rule: start from current balance, subtract known planned payments before the next planned income, divide the rest by days until that income (example: 12000 on the 1st, 7000 utilities on the 7th, next income 15000 on the 20th)
- (insight) landscape research: daily safe-to-spend is standard (Monzo Left to spend, Simple Safe-to-Spend, Copilot Free to Spend, PocketGuard Leftover, Cleo Daily Plan); formula is commodity; company-grade P&L/CF/balance for individuals is a niche (PocketSmith, Keel)
- (decision) daily limit = actual balance minus all planned outflows in the window / days to next income; example yields 263/day not 384; recomputed daily and after each transaction; horizon ends at next income but reserves any deficit beyond it
- (decision) cash basis: expected income is excluded until it actually arrives; rollover of unspent daily limit is automatic because the base is the real balance, no separate counter
- (note) monetization deliberately deferred: first a working product, then sell; user says nobody would pay for the current feature set
- (assumption) user assumes current functionality is not sellable; commercial value must come from something not yet built (unverified)
- (note) user's definition of a working product: (1) liabilities - credits, loans, splits; (2) safe-to-spend; (3) analytics with charts exposing waste, e.g. 7000/month on coffee; (4) investments - deposits and interest; (5) third-party money tracking: parents' 3000 for a shelf, recorded but neither income nor expense
- (insight) the five features map onto company statements: balance sheet (accounts + liabilities + deposits), P&L (categories + waste analytics), cash flow (safe-to-spend + calendar), plus a fourth boundary - whose money is it (third-party funds)
- (decision) priority for the next 3 months: liabilities accounting first, because credit mistakes are the most common user error
- (decision) rework of existing UI: redesign the main screen, remove the nearest-payments block
- (insight) liabilities are not a separate module: they are a new class of mandatory reserve inside the same engine that computes safe-to-spend; the feature is reserve + limit + savings from early repayment
- (decision) v1 liability types: mortgage and consumer credit only; credit cards, splits and informal debts are out of scope
- (decision) liability form: no payment calculation in-app, user takes the payment from the contract; fields = type (radio: consumer/mortgage), name, loan amount, annual rate, monthly payment
- (decision) liability form adds paid amount and payment day; remaining debt = total minus paid; the payment is auto-added to the payment calendar
- (decision) liability field is remaining principal (copied from the bank), not amount paid; paid amount is derived and labeled as a calculation
- (decision) payment split is automatic: interest = remaining x rate/12, principal = payment - interest; cash flow loses the full payment, liability drops by principal, P&L gets interest only; manual correction of remaining balance is allowed at any time
- (assumption) if a payment includes insurance or fees, the derived interest share will be slightly off; documented as a known limitation
- (decision) waste analytics: user marks categories as optional or bad (alcohol, cigarettes = bad; coffee = wants); user sets a monthly budget for such spending and checks whether he is inside it - envelope rule
- (decision) budgets are per category (groceries 5, entertainment 2, ...), not envelopes; a budget cannot be created without real money behind it - budget planning is validated against actual balances
- (decision) budgets are blocked when their sum exceeds free cash (balance minus reserves); budgets get their own screen and are anchored to the payment calendar
- (decision) parked: investments/deposits accounting deferred, to be decided later
- (decision) dropped: third-party money separation - judged too complex, out of scope
- (decision) success criteria: no borrowing before payday; user clearly sees what is left and how much can be spent per day
- (note) user base today: author only; commercial intent remains a hypothesis, no validated market
- (note) main screen spec: total balance with expandable accounts list; income/expense block with period switch (day/week/month/quarter/year/custom); safe-to-spend today inside that block; nearest payments move to the payment calendar; short operations list with a link to all; donut chart of category shares with all/income/expense toggles
- (note) user has no answer yet on why anyone would choose this over Zen-Money or a spreadsheet; will be stated honestly in the brief
